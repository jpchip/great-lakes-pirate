## Snapshot: what's happening in spring 1900

Useful as world-state flavor, rumors and newspaper headlines at game start.

- **The Duluth Ship Canal and Superior Entry piers are being rebuilt in concrete** (1896–1902), part of a $3M federal harbor project that also dredges 20-ft channels. ✅ *Game:* construction sites, scaffolding, temporary lights. Good night-theft targets (tools, iron, cement).
- **The Duluth South Breakwater light is a temporary tower** (1898–1900) while the pier is rebuilt. The permanent tower is finished August 1901. ✅
- **Devils Island's permanent tower stands, but its big third-order lens doesn't arrive until 1901.** ✅ (lens arrived April 1901) *Game:* a weaker light is a known gap in coverage on the exposed north edge of the Apostles. A prime wrecking spot.
- **The American Steel Barge Co. (whalebacks) is sold to American Ship Building Co. in 1900.** ✅ *Game:* a shipyard in transition; rumors of layoffs and surplus parts.
- **Bayfield fishing is dominated by one big packer** (the real Booth Fisheries; fictionalized in-game). Its cooperage was built around 1900. ✅
- **Island brownstone quarries are abandoned** (the last one closed in 1897). ✅
- **A lumber company has been logging Bad River reservation timber since 1894**, with a mill at Odanah. ✅ (federal record; oral history describes company-store credit). *Game:* background context only. Odanah stays a trade-only community (no crime).
- Older, now-dark **Minnesota Point lighthouse** (lit 1858, extinguished 1885 per most sources; ⚠️ one source says 1913). *Game:* an abandoned lighthouse at the Superior Entry end of Minnesota Point. A great secret or hideout candidate.

**Implications for plan 03 (hybrid scale):**
- Natural **coastal zones**: (1) Duluth–Superior harbor (~6 nm across), (2) Two Harbors (small), (3) Siskiwit Bay (small), (4) the Apostles and Chequamegon Bay (~30 × 30 nm, the main playground).

## Port Profiles

### Duluth, Minnesota
- **Population 1900:** 52,969 (20,983 foreign-born). ✅
- **Economy:** grain elevators, coal docks, ore docks, sawmills, rail hub. Duluth and Superior together logged ~5,400 vessel arrivals in 1895. ✅ (1895 figure)
- **Landmarks:** Duluth Ship Canal (cut 1871; piers under concrete reconstruction 1896–1902) ✅, South Breakwater light (temporary tower in 1900) ✅, Life-Saving Station on Minnesota Point (built ~1894, operating by 1895) ⚠️, abandoned Minnesota Point lighthouse ⚠️. *Not yet present:* the aerial bridge (1905), the North Pier light (1910).
- **Law:** city police, St. Louis County sheriff, federal presence (US Marshal, customs). ❓
- **Market (draft):** sells grain, flour, coal, manufactured goods; buys lumber, fish, ore, furs. Best prices, biggest volume.
- **Game:** largest market, **best fences** (crowded and anonymous), heaviest policing. Strong candidate for the second docks district (big and complex). Canal construction sites offer night theft. The canal is a chokepoint where you're very visible.

### Superior, Wisconsin
- **Population 1900:** 31,091 (up from 11,983 in 1890). Second-largest city in Wisconsin. ✅ (local sources)
- **Economy:** shipbuilding (whaleback yard, sold to American Ship Building in 1900), ore, coal and grain docks, rail. ✅/❓
- **Landmarks:** Superior Entry (the natural harbor mouth; new concrete piers 1896–1902) ✅, the shipyard ✅, Wisconsin Point. The Superior Entry light setup in 1900 is ❓ (one source says a new pierhead light was built in 1885 when the Minnesota Point light was retired).
- **Law:** Superior police, Douglas County. A **separate Wanted region from Duluth** (decided).
- **Game:** **shipyard services** (best upgrades and hull purchases), whaleback traffic, an escape hatch from Duluth heat across the harbor.

### Two Harbors (Agate Bay), Minnesota
- **Population 1900:** ❓
- **Economy:** iron ore. The Duluth & Iron Range Railroad built the first ore dock in **1884** for Vermilion Range ore. ✅ Rail shops. *Not yet present:* the concrete Dock #6 (1907–08).
- **Landmarks:** ore docks (wooden in 1900) ✅. **Two Harbors Light, first lit April 14/15, 1892**, red-brick light station with a **fourth-order Fresnel** lens and a fog signal. ✅
- **Game:** a rough company town. Ore boats depart loaded, so they're heavy, slow and valuable in bulk but low value per ton. A small docks area (rail yard, ore dock, saloon). The railroad company is a fictionalized target.

### Ashland, Wisconsin
- **Population 1900:** 13,074 (4,417 foreign-born). ✅ (1911 Britannica)
- **Economy:** **Gogebic Range iron ore**. The first ore dock was built in 1884–85 by the Milwaukee, Lake Shore & Western Railway. ✅ Several wooden ore docks by 1900 ⚠️. Lumber mills, brownstone (Ashland's brownstone buildings). *Not yet present:* the giant concrete Soo Line dock (1916).
- **Game:** a busy Chequamegon Bay hub with ore and lumber. Mid-sized docks district candidate.

### Washburn, Wisconsin
- **Population 1900:** ❓
- **Economy:** the railroad favored Washburn as a port over Bayfield. Lumber, coal and rail docks. ✅ (direction) / ❓ (details)
- **Game:** a rail and lumber port. Good legit cargo contracts (lumber), and coal for steamers (coal theft?).

### Bayfield, Wisconsin
- **Population 1900:** ❓ (Bayfield County total: 14,392). ✅
- **Economy:** **commercial fishing**: the big packer opened in 1880 and employed up to 500 at its peak; a cooperage was built around 1900 to pack fish. ✅ Lumber mills (e.g., Pike and Knight, 125 workers in the 1880s). ✅ The railroad arrived in the late 1880s. ✅ Many Red Cliff Ojibwe worked in Bayfield's fishing industry. ✅
- **Landmarks:** fish docks, packing house, cooperage, sawmills, the gateway to the Apostles. Raspberry and La Pointe lights nearby.
- **Game:** **first docks-district candidate** (compact, distinctive, close to the starting area). The fictionalized fishing company HQ is the main rival. Fish is the core cheap good. A natural home port for a new player.

### La Pointe (Madeline Island)
- **History:** an old fur-trade post. **The 1854 Treaty of La Pointe was signed here.** A central place in Ojibwe history. ✅
- **Winter:** Chequamegon Bay generally freezes between the mainland and the nearest islands, and an **ice road** links Bayfield and La Pointe in mid to late winter. Mail historically crossed by dog sled, and there was a documented death on the ice in February 1858. ✅
- **Game:** a small village, a winter ice-road smuggling route, a historic atmosphere. Rumors and lore.

### Siskiwit Bay / Cornucopia
- **History:** an Ojibwe stopover on the way to La Pointe ("Siskiwit" refers to a lake trout). The first settlers were loggers in the late 1800s, **lumbering ended around 1900**, and the herring-fishing boom came only **after 1909**. ✅ Whether the name "Cornucopia" was in use in 1900 is ❓.
- **Game:** **in 1900 this is a fading logging landing, not a fishing village.** Recommendation: call it **Siskiwit Bay** in-game, a near-empty landing with an abandoned logging camp and a couple of fishermen. A good spot for a smuggler's cove or hideout, with sea caves nearby. (Updates the master plan's port list.)

### Red Cliff (Ojibwe community) — trade only
- **History:** founded by the part of the La Pointe band that followed Chief Buffalo after the 1854 treaty (largely Catholic). Many members worked in Bayfield's commercial fishing. ✅
- **Game:** a trading post with seasonal goods (see §Ojibwe trade goods). No crime interactions.

### Bad River / Odanah (Ojibwe community) — trade only
- **History:** settled by those keeping Midewiwin traditions after the treaty split. ✅ From 1894 a lumber company held federal authority to log reservation timber, with a mill at Odanah. Oral history describes the company "running the town" and paying in company-store credit. ✅ In 1901, John Blackbird was arrested for netting fish on the reservation, and a federal court ruled against Wisconsin's authority. ✅
- **Location:** Odanah is on the Bad River, inland from the lake. The river mouth and sloughs are reached by boat. ❓ (navigability for a schooner; probably a small-boat or landing trip)
- **Game:** a trading partner. Reaching it may be by rowboat or landing rather than docking the schooner (plan 03). No crime interactions.

#### Ojibwe trade goods (seasonal, draft)
From general sources on the Lake Superior Ojibwe seasonal round: **fish year-round, game and wild rice in the fall, maple sugar in late winter and spring**, plus furs. ✅ (general, not specific to 1900 at Red Cliff and Bad River)

| Good | Season available | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fish (fresh/salted) | Year-round | Competes with the packer |
| Wild rice | Fall | High value, seasonal |
| Maple sugar | Late winter – spring | High value, seasonal |
| Furs | Winter | Ties into winter activities |
| Crafted goods | ❓ | Verify with tribal sources before including |
