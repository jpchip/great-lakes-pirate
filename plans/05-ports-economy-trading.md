## Purpose

Define money, goods, markets, legit contracts, fences and black markets, port services, the traffic simulation that fills the lake with ships worth robbing, and the information economy (rumors and price knowledge). It also implements the decided rule that **a legit trading-only path is viable but harder**.

---

## 1. Goals

1. **Two viable incomes:** honest freight and trade (slow, safe) and piracy and theft (fast, hot). Margins make the choice meaningful.
2. **The economy creates targets.** Every ship you meet carries a real manifest generated by real cargo flows.
3. **Logistics matter.** Per-hideout storage, cargo capacity, seasons and distance shape the decisions.
4. **No infinite money exploits.** Selling floods markets, prices recover over time, and stolen goods carry heat.
5. **Simple to tune.** A small number of goods, readable formulas, and data-driven tables.

---

## 2. Money

- Currency: **US dollars**, stored as **integer cents**. Prices are period-plausible in *ratios*, not exact history.
- **Starting purse:** ~$100–200 depending on background, plus a T1 schooner (owned outright) and a small starting cargo (also background-dependent).
- Money is physical: **gold and cash on hand** (carried on the ship or person) vs. **stashed** at hideouts (stored per hideout, like goods). **Decided:** cash on hand is **lost** with the ship if you abandon ship, and **confiscated** on capture. Stashed money is safe.
- **No bank (decided).** There's no bank or other mechanic that freezes your money. Hideout stashes are the only safe storage, which reinforces hideout logistics.

## 4. Markets

### 4.1 Model (per port, per good)
- State: `stock`, `targetStock`, `basePrice`, `production/day`, `consumption/day`, `seasonalModifier(date)`.
- Price: `price = basePrice × seasonalMod × (targetStock / max(stock, ε))^elasticity`, clamped to [0.4×, 3×] of base.
  - **Spread:** the port sells to you at price × 1.08 and buys from you at price × 0.92 (smaller spread in big ports).
- **Player impact:** selling adds to stock (price drops immediately), buying removes it (price rises). Stock drifts back toward target on the world tick, so **dumping a full hold crashes the local price**. Spreading sales across ports pays.
- **Events** modify markets: a storm sinks a grain boat (Duluth grain price up), a mill fire (lumber up), a herring run (fish down), winter freeze (provisions up at island villages).

### 4.3 Price knowledge (information economy)
- You only know **current** prices in the port you're in. Other ports show **last-seen prices with a date** in the logbook ("Duluth, whitefish $14, 2 weeks ago").
- Rumors, newspapers and the telegraph office (paid, in big ports) update price knowledge.
- Background perk hooks: the *Saloon Keeper* gets better rumors, and the *Fisherman* knows fish prices everywhere.

## 5. Legit Trade & Contracts

### 5.1 Speculative trade
Buy low and sell high across ports. With the T1's small hold, profits are modest (~$20–60 per run early). That's slow but steady.

### 5.2 Freight contracts
- Offered by the **freight agent / harbor master** in each port: "Carry 15 CU of salt from Superior to Bayfield by the week of June 4. Pays $90. Deposit $30."
- Variants: rush delivery (bonus), fragile goods, passenger ferrying (later tiers), mail (good pay and reputation), **company contracts** with the fictional firms.
- **Standing** (a legit reputation, per port and per company, 0–100) rises with on-time deliveries. Higher Standing unlocks better contracts, lower dock fees, credit for deposits, and shipyard discounts.
- **Decided:** Standing is a separate legit reputation, independent of Fame.
- **The Seavey temptation (decided: allowed once per company):** contract cargo is in your hold, and you can simply **steal it** (sell it elsewhere). That's a crime against the company: Standing with it drops to zero permanently, it **never offers you contracts again**, and Wanted rises (it's always traced; they know who carried it). It's a big one-time payout that burns that bridge for good.

### 5.3 Legit-path balance targets
| Milestone | Legit only | Mixed / piracy |
|---|---|---|
| T2 schooner affordable | ~8–12 real hours | ~3–5 real hours |
| T3 affordable | ~20–30 real hours | ~8–12 real hours |
| Typical profit per voyage (T1) | $20–60 | $50–300 (with heat) |

Harder but viable. A legit player avoids all heat, gets the best contracts, and can trade in every port freely. These are tuning targets for Phase 4 playtests.

## 6. Stolen Goods, Heat & Fences

### 6.1 "Hot" goods
- Goods obtained by crime are flagged **hot** with `{region, heatLevel, traceability, since}`.
- **Selling hot goods to a legit merchant** in the **same region** carries a **detection chance**. It's scaled by heat, traceability (company-marked crates are worse) and merchant type, and reduced by distance in time and space.
- Heat on goods **cools** over time (weeks) and is effectively **gone in a different region** for low-traceability goods (loose grain, fish). Marked goods stay recognizable longer, and re-crating them at a hideout workshop (a plan 09 recipe) removes the markings.
- **Detection** when selling: the merchant refuses or alerts the law (+Wanted), and the goods may be confiscated.

### 6.2 Fences & black markets
- **Fences** operate in **docks districts at night** (saloon back rooms, a warehouse, a fish shed). They buy hot goods and contraband with no detection risk at **40–70% of value**, depending on Fame tier, the fence's relationship with you, and current heat.
- Limits: each fence has **weekly cash** and good-type preferences (the Duluth fence takes anything; the Bayfield fence wants fish and furs).
- **Unlocks by Fame** (plan 06): Unknown players know one small fence (Bayfield). Higher Fame unlocks the Duluth fence and better rates, then smugglers who *sell* contraband and special goods.
- **Informants:** at high Wanted, a fence may sell you out (a small chance per visit, higher if you've wronged them). That keeps tension.

### 6.3 Smuggling
- **Untaxed whiskey** and other contraband are bought from smugglers or distilled at a hideout (plan 09) and sold to saloons and lumber and mining camps for big margins. Selling openly is illegal.
- **Customs inspections:** the cutter and harbor police can inspect cargo (plan 06). Hidden compartments (plan 10) conceal some CU.
- Expansion hook: the Canadian shore (Port Arthur / Fort William) becomes a customs-smuggling destination once that region is added.

## 7. Port Services

| Service | Where | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Market / merchants** | All ports | Buy and sell goods |
| **Freight agent / harbor master** | All except tiny landings | Contracts, dock fees, clearance papers, Standing |
| **Shipyard** | Superior (best), Duluth, Ashland | Buy ships, upgrades, full repairs (plan 10) |
| **Chandlery** | Most ports | Repair materials, rope and canvas, powder and shot, lanterns, provisions |
| **Saloon** | All ports | Rumors (buy drinks), jobs, fences (at night), whiskey |
| **Telegraph office** | Duluth, Superior, Ashland | Buy price updates; news |
| **Passage office** | Duluth, Superior, Ashland, Bayfield, Washburn | Steamer tickets between ports when shipless ([02 §6.5](02-arcade-sailing.md)) |
| **Police station / jail** | Duluth, Superior, Ashland (others have a constable) | Bail, fines, bribes (plan 06) |
| **Harbor tug tow** | Duluth, Superior, Ashland, Two Harbors | Tows a ship in or out of the harbor in calms (mainly T3, which has no sweeps; [02 §3.6](02-arcade-sailing.md)). Small fee; game time passes |

**Dock fees:** a small daily berth fee in big ports, free at anchor. **Provisions:** a light upkeep (cents per day aboard), auto-deducted when docked (cut list).

## 8. Traffic Simulation (where targets come from)

### 8.1 Cargo flows
`data/flows.json` defines seasonal flows: origin → destination, good, volume per week and ship classes. Examples:

| Flow | Ships | Season peak |
|---|---|---|
| Ore: Two Harbors, Duluth, Superior, Ashland → "lower lakes" (exits east off the MVP chart) | Steel bulk freighters, whalebacks, schooner-barges under tow | Sep–Nov |
| Grain: Duluth–Superior → lower lakes | Steel freighters, wooden steamers | Sep–Nov |
| Coal: lower lakes → Duluth, Superior, Ashland, Washburn | Freighters (inbound) | All season |
| Lumber: Washburn, Ashland, Bayfield → lower lakes and Duluth | Schooners, lumber hookers, barges | May–Oct |
| Fish: Apostles fish camps → Bayfield → Duluth | Fishing tugs, small steamers | Spring and fall runs |
| Passenger and package: Duluth ↔ Bayfield ↔ Ashland (scheduled) | Passenger steamers | Jun–Sep |
| Local: tugs in harbors, fishing tugs around the Apostles | Tugs | Daily |

- The **traffic generator** spawns **abstract voyages** on the route graph ([03 §6](03-world-scale-chart-travel.md)) to satisfy flows. Each ship gets a **manifest** (goods, value, owner company, crew, armament) consistent with the flow.
- Ships delivering to a port actually **change that port's stock** (inbound coal raises the Duluth coal stock), so the economy and traffic are one system.
- "Lower lakes" is a **sink/source node** at the east edge of the MVP chart: ships leave and return after a delay. Lake Michigan and the Soo replace it in expansions.

### 8.2 Crime feedback
- Robbing ships reduces deliveries, so destination prices rise (and fences pay more for those goods. Neat).
- **Company grudge** (per fictional company, 0–100): rises when you hit its ships, warehouses or contracts. **Decided: companies do not arm up.** There are no hired guards, decoy ships or company bounties. Grudge only affects the **legit relationship**: the company refuses contracts, its merchants charge you worse prices and its agents are more likely to notice its marked goods (raising detection when you sell its hot goods). Grudge decays slowly.

### 8.3 Special targets
- **Payroll shipments** (cash to lumber and mining camps), **mail**, and **valuable passengers**: rare, high value, very high heat. Learned through rumors and timed.

## 9. Rumors & Information

- **Sources:** saloon drinks ($0.25–$2), newspapers (Fame, events, prices), the telegraph office, Ojibwe trading posts (local weather and ice knowledge only, given respectfully), passing ships (hailing, in daylight).
- **Rumor types:** shipment tips (ship, cargo, departure window), price tips, cutter whereabouts, fence locations, island legends and secret leads (plan 11), weather outlook.
- Rumors carry **reliability** (some are false or stale) and an **age**. The logbook keeps them.

## 11. Data & State

**Data files:** `goods.json`, `markets.json` (per-port profiles), `flows.json`, `contracts.json` (templates), `fences.json`, `factions.json` (companies plus grudge rules), `services.json`, `rumors.json` (templates).

- `contracts.active[]`

- `knowledge.rumors[]`
- `traffic.voyages[]` (abstract ships with manifests)
- `fences[id].{cash, relation}`

**Events:** `TradeCompleted`, `ContractAccepted/Completed/Failed/Stolen`, `HotGoodsDetected`, `FenceSold`, `GrudgeChanged`, `PriceShock {port, good, cause}`, `VoyageSpawned/Completed`.

- The economy tick stays within budget with 100+ abstract voyages.
