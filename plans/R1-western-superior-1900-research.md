# R1 — Research: Western Lake Superior, circa 1900

Status: DRAFT v0.4 (high-priority research pass done; see also [R1a gazetteer](R1a-western-superior-gazetteer.md))
Parent: [00-master-plan.md](00-master-plan.md)
Feeds: 03 (world/scale), 05 (economy), 06 (law/lighthouses), 07 (targets), 08 (docks districts), 11 (islands/secrets), 12 (events/backgrounds), 14 (art)

## Purpose

Build a reliable, game-ready picture of the MVP region (western Lake Superior: the Apostle Islands, Chequamegon Bay, Duluth–Superior, and the North Shore to Two Harbors) as it was in **1900**. Separate what is verified, what conflicts between sources, and what is unverified, then turn the research into concrete game content: ports, landmarks, ships, goods, factions, hazards and secrets.

This doc is **not** a history paper. Every finding should end in a "so what for the game" note.

### Accuracy policy (from master plan)
- "1900-flavored," not strict. Gameplay wins ties, but deviations get logged (see §8).
- Real places, landmarks and natural features use real names.
- **Companies (decided):** companies the player can attack or rob (fisheries, railroads, ore and lumber firms, shipping lines) are **fictionalized** analogs. Neutral landmarks (towns, lighthouses, islands, natural features, government services) keep their **real names**.
- **Ships (decided):** mostly fictional names, plus **a few real ships as Easter eggs** (e.g., *Sevona*, *Noquebay*, the cutter *Morrill*).
- Ojibwe communities: accuracy and respect are **not** negotiable. That topic gets a stricter bar than the rest (see §5).

## 2. Preliminary Findings

### 2.1 Lighthouses (Apostle Islands and vicinity)

All of these exist by 1900, which confirms that the move to a 1900 start year gives the full set.

Lens order sets beam strength. Fresnel orders run from 1st (largest, longest range) to 6th (smallest). For the game, map lens order to a **light tier** (sight and beam radius) rather than exact nautical miles.

| Light | Built / first lit | Lens (original) | State in spring 1900 | Confidence | Game notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| La Pointe (original, Madeline Island area) | 1852 | — | Gone (replaced) | ✅ | Lore only. |
| Michigan Island | 1856–57 | 3½-order since 1869; characteristic ❓ | Active | ✅ / ⚠️ date | Poorly sited per historical accounts ("the unwanted lighthouse"). **Tier: strong.** |
| La Pointe light (Long Island) | 1858 | ❓ | Active | ✅ / ❓ lens | Guards the Chequamegon Bay approach. |
| Raspberry Island | 1862; lit July 20, 1863 | 5th-order; **white flash every 90 s** around 1900 | Active | ✅ | West channel, near Bayfield. **Tier: weak beam, but fast reporting** (close to town). |
| Outer Island | 1874 | 3rd-order Sautter; **white flash every 90 s** | Active | ✅ | Most remote; tall tower. **Tier: strong**, slow reporting. |
| Sand Island | 1881; lit Sept 25, 1881 | 4th-order Lepaute, **fixed white** (until 1921) | Active | ✅ | Brownstone building (art reference). **Tier: medium.** Guards the *Sevona* shoal. |
| Devils Island | 1891 temporary; permanent tower ~1898 | 1891–1901: temporary **fixed red**, 4th-order. 3rd-order lens **arrives June 22, 1901**, in service **Sept 20, 1901** (alternating red and white flashes every 10 s) | **Tower up, temporary fixed red light (weak)** | ✅ | **A known coverage gap in 1900**: a prime wrecking spot on the exposed north edge. Sea caves. |
| Chequamegon Point | 1896–97 | ❓ | Active | ✅ / ❓ lens | Tip of Long Island; erosion problems. |
| Duluth South Breakwater Outer (canal south pier) | 1874; current tower Aug 1901 | ❓ | **Temporary tower** (pier being rebuilt 1898–1901) | ✅ | Canal entrance light amid construction. |
| Duluth North Pier | 1910 | — | **Not built** (pier unlit) | ✅ | Exclude. |
| Minnesota Point (Superior Entry end) | 1858 | — | **Dark/abandoned** (extinguished 1885; one source says 1913) | ⚠️ | **Abandoned lighthouse**: secret or hideout candidate. |
| Superior Entry pierhead | ~1885 (replaced Minnesota Point) | ❓ | Likely active; piers being rebuilt | ❓ | Verify. |
| Two Harbors (Agate Bay) | Lit April 14/15, 1892 | 4th-order Fresnel; fog signal | Active | ✅ | **Tier: medium.** Red-brick station. |

**Light tiers (proposal for plan 06):** strong (3rd/3½-order), medium (4th), weak (5th and temporary lights). Tier sets beam and sight radius. Distance to town sets reporting speed.

**Game implications**
- There are roughly **8–11 lighthouses** in the MVP region. Each one is a detection node, a raidable site, and a possible wrecking (moon cussing) setup.
- Design each with a sight radius, a keeper count and a signal method. These values feed plan 06.
- Remote lights (Outer, Devils, Michigan) give low reporting speed but high value for wrecking. Lights near Bayfield (Raspberry, La Pointe) report fast.

### 2.2 Law Enforcement & Rescue

| Finding | Confidence | Game notes |
|---|---|---|
| The service was renamed from "Revenue-Marine" to **US Revenue Cutter Service** in 1894 | ✅ | Use "Revenue Cutter Service" in-game. |
| Around 1901, **USRC *Morrill*** covered Lakes Michigan **and** Superior plus the St. Marys River; USRC *Fessenden* covered Erie/Huron | ✅ (single trade-press source, but specific) | Historically there was **about one cutter for two lakes**. Real patrols were sparse. |
| The *Morrill* was **based at Milwaukee** after August 1898 and **laid up in winter** | ✅ | No cutter on Superior in winter ([04](04-water-weather-seasons.md)). |
| **US Life-Saving Service** station at Duluth (Minnesota Point), **built 1894, manned 1895** | ✅ (how it reported to town, e.g., telephone, is ❓) | Surfmen are witnesses who report and rescue, not fighters. A lifeboat can shadow you. |
| Life-Saving Service merged into the Coast Guard in 1915 | ✅ | Confirms there's no "Coast Guard" in 1900. |
| Other western Superior life-saving stations circa 1900 (Bayfield? Two Harbors?) | ❓ | Research. |
| Local law in ports (city police, county sheriffs, US Marshals in Duluth/Superior) | ❓ | Research the structure only; NPCs will be fictional. |

**Game implications**
- **Decided: keep patrols historically sparse.** One revenue cutter (the *Morrill*, a real-ship Easter egg) roams Lakes Michigan and Superior. Because it covers two lakes, it isn't always nearby. This is what makes a one-person pirate feasible.
  - The cutter's presence becomes a **knowable, trackable threat**: rumors in saloons, sightings, newspaper reports of "the *Morrill* coaling at Duluth." Planning around its whereabouts is part of the game.
  - Most day-to-day danger comes from **watchers and local law**: lighthouse keepers, life-saving surfmen, harbor police and constables, armed freighter crews, and the docks-district watch.
  - At high regional Wanted, the *Morrill* **prioritizes your region** and spends more time hunting there. At the top tier, a temporary second vessel (a chartered tug with deputies, or a borrowed cutter) may join. This is a small, explainable escalation rather than a fleet. Details are for plan 06.

## 5. Ojibwe Communities (special handling)

### Findings so far
- **Treaty of La Pointe, signed 30 September 1854 on Madeline Island**, created permanent reservations in Wisconsin after the US government's failed removal attempts (including the Sandy Lake tragedy of 1850). ✅
- The La Pointe band divided: one group (largely Catholic) followed **Chief Buffalo (Kechewaishke)** to **Red Cliff**, and those keeping traditional Midewiwin practice settled at **Bad River**. ✅ (secondary sources; deepen)
- The treaty reserved the right to **hunt, fish and gather on ceded lands**, but those rights were often ignored by the state. In **1901**, John Blackbird was arrested for netting fish on the Bad River Reservation, and a federal court ruled against Wisconsin's authority there. ✅
- Around 1900, many community members worked for wage employers in Bayfield, notably in commercial fishing. Federal authorities allowed lumber companies to cut much of the reservation timber around the turn of the century. ✅ (needs better sources)
- Madeline Island (La Pointe) is historically a central and sacred place in Ojibwe history (Moningwunakauning). ❓ Get the spelling and meaning from tribal sources.
- **Bad River, 1894 on:** a lumber company received federal authority (January 1894) to cut reservation timber and opened a mill at Odanah in September 1894. Oral history (PBS Wisconsin) describes the company "running the town" and paying workers in company-store credit. ✅
- **Seasonal round (general Lake Superior Ojibwe):** fish year-round, game and wild rice in the fall, maple sugar in late winter and spring, furs. ✅ (general; confirm specifics for 1900 Red Cliff and Bad River with tribal sources). This drives the seasonal trade goods table in the gazetteer.

### Design guidance (draft)
- Portray Red Cliff and Bad River as **real, living, independent communities** in 1900, under real pressures (treaty violations, timber loss, wage labor, fishing rights disputes). They are not fantasy "natives," not mystical quest-givers, and never generic enemies.
- **Decided: no crimes against Ojibwe communities.** The game doesn't offer theft, attacks or wrecking against them. Their villages, boats and people aren't valid targets, and crime interactions are simply unavailable there (not just penalized).
- **Decided: interaction is trading.** Red Cliff and Bad River act as trading posts or markets with their own goods and demands (e.g., fish, wild rice, maple sugar, furs, crafted goods; ❓ verify against sources). Keep this simple and respectful. Anything beyond trading (guides, lore) is out of scope unless revisited later.
- Use Ojibwe names and language carefully: verify spellings and get permission-level care for anything cultural or spiritual. Avoid depicting sacred ceremonies.
- **Pre-release:** seek review from Red Cliff and Bad River cultural or historic preservation offices, or Ojibwe consultants. Budget for it, even as a solo developer (possibly a paid sensitivity review).

### Sources to consult (priority)
- Red Cliff Band and Bad River Band official sites, and their Tribal Historic Preservation Offices.
- Great Lakes Indian Fish & Wildlife Commission (GLIFWC): treaty rights history.
- Madeline Island Museum (Wisconsin Historical Society).
- Wisconsin Historical Society essays on the 1854 treaty.
- Chequamegon History (chequamegonhistory.com): a local primary-source archive.
